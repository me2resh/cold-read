## 1. The answer in one paragraph

**The short answer**

- Not on the offered terms: Dunmarrow can deliver from its first depot and is growing, but the record does not support a three-year exclusive with it 🟡, because the facts in the next group are each cited and the weighing of them is this note's [^s1-1][^s1-9][^s1-6][^s6-4].

**The evidence**

- Turnover rose from GBP 52.8m to GBP 61.4m in the year to 31 December 2025 while gross margin fell from 14.0 to 12.5 per cent, and the accounts were filed 35 days late ✅ [^s1-1][^s1-2][^s1-3].
- It reported an average of 58 days to pay suppliers in the six months to 30 June 2026, against published terms of 30 days, with 47 per cent of invoices not paid within agreed terms ✅ [^s1-9][^s2-2].
- An investment firm's fund has held a majority of the parent, Dunmarrow Holdings Ltd, since 28 June 2024, and a lender holds a charge over the vehicles and book debts ✅ [^s1-6][^s1-4].
- It launched an own-label yoghurt range on 12 May 2025 in a category where it had distributed Larchmantle since 2021, and it is advertising for an own-label product developer and a category buyer for chilled meals ✅ [^s3-2][^s2-4][^s6-4][^s6-5].
- Its storage and distribution certificate, read on the issuer's register, covers the first depot only ✅ [^s2-6].

**The terms**

- A term of about a year, exclusivity by territory in return for volume commitments, payment held to the published 30 days, an own-label non-compete in chilled meals, a change-of-control exit and certification of the second depot before use 🟡, each tied to a cited fact in §9 [^s1-9][^s6-5][^s1-7][^s2-6].
- Three things stay unknown and are for Dunmarrow to answer openly: the margin it would take, its customer concentration and the investment firm's holding period ❔ not public; not in the accounts, the supplier terms page or the firm's release [^s1-1][^s2-2][^s1-12].

## 9. Terms to ask for and questions to put openly

**Terms**

1. A first term of 12 months, renewable by agreement, in place of three years: the lender's facilities fall due in June 2027 and the majority owner is an investment firm, so lender and owner can both change inside a longer term ✅ [^s1-1][^s1-6].
2. Non-exclusive, or exclusive only in the first depot's delivery area: the certificate on the issuer's register covers that depot and no other ✅ [^s2-6].
3. Volume commitments in return for any exclusivity, which lapses if a quarter is missed: Dunmarrow already carries Yarrowmere in the same category, and its published terms commit it to no volume ✅ [^s2-4][^s2-2].
4. Payment within the 30 days Dunmarrow publishes, with a right to suspend supply and end exclusivity after a second late payment: it reported an average of 58 days and 47 per cent of invoices outside agreed terms ✅ [^s1-9].
5. A credit limit and retention of title until paid: the lender's charge over book debts ranks ahead of trade creditors ✅ [^s1-4].
6. No own-label chilled ready meals in the territory during the term and for 12 months after, and no use of the supplier's sales data for own-label work: the yoghurt launch of 2025 and the two ads of 2026 ✅ [^s3-2][^s6-4][^s6-5].
7. A right to end the agreement on 90 days' notice if the company or its parent changes owner: a fund holds 68 per cent of the parent ✅ [^s1-7].
8. No product through the second depot until it appears on the issuer's register: the scheme certifies site by site ✅ [^s4-7][^s2-6].
9. The named account manager, the yearly joint growth plan and monthly sales by shop written into the agreement: no supplier-facing role is open, and the 2023 no-compete sentence has gone from the programme page ✅ [^s6-1][^s3-6].
