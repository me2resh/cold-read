# Output templates

Pick by lens. Every template starts with the same two lines under the title: the date and the legend. Every section ends with its sources, or the note has a sources section by lane.

## A. Outside view of a deal or integration (deal lens)

```
# <Acquirer>, <Target A> and <Target B>: the integration, from the outside
Read on <date>. Public record only. Legend: ✅ 🟡 ❔

## 1. The answer in one paragraph
## 2. The deals, side by side        (table: announced, price and structure, what was bought, purchase price allocation, what the allocation says, earn-out status, impairment)
## 3. What it says and what it does  (quotes with dates; actions with dates; the brand plan as far as it can be read)
## 4. The integration so far         (dated timeline, one line per event)
## 5. The platform picture           (what runs where; shared rails; migration notices; identity and vendor tenants; what a cutover would look like)
## 6. The openings as a plan         (counts; five ads that matter; predictions by horizon; ten roles that would tell the most)
## 7. The outside view and the base rate   (analyst split; regulatory climate; comparables table; general pattern; what a unit inside should expect 🟡)
## 8. What it means for <the unit or company>   (assets kept and for how long; the likely window 🟡; surviving roles 🟡)
## 9. What it means for <the reader>           (working copy only; removed from the shareable copy)
## 10. Legal pointer                             (where employees are affected; a pointer, not advice)
## 11. Signals to watch, dated                  (table: date, event, what to look for, source)
## 12. Sources by lane
```

## B. Stack by chapter (join lens)

```
# <Company> engineering stack by chapter (research, <date>)
## The platform in one paragraph
## 1..N. One chapter per engineering area  (table per chapter: component | what it does | what it runs on today | confidence | where it is heading; then the ads that name the stack)
## Platform, infrastructure, DevOps and QA  (cross-cutting)
## Regulatory, quality and security         (what the product's regulated status demands of engineering)
## Org and ways of working                  (teams, locations, cadence, process from ads and talks)
## Leadership                               (from the team page and filings; link to the people note)
## Market                                   (who else does this; pricing where public)
## Money                                    (from lane 1: runway, going concern, raise history)
## What it means for the <role> mandate     (what it owns, what it inherits, what is broken, what the first quarter must deliver)
## Vocabulary to have ready
## Questions by round
## Job ads and the stack each one names
## Sources
```

## E. Position brief (position lens)

```
# <Company> position in <market> (<date>)
## The answer in one paragraph
## Market map            (named competitors; pricing where public; channels)
## The moat, as the record shows it   (allocation, channels, licences, data, brand)
## Regulatory climate    (the real constraint, dated)
## Says versus does
## Three dated events that would change the ranking
## Sources
```

## Writing rules for all templates

- Plain sentences; a table where items are parallel. No marketing adjectives; no claims without a mark.
- Real names for companies, brands and executives named in public filings or press. Initials or no names for staff below executive level and for anyone known only from inside.
- Say "not public" rather than estimating when a number is missing; name the one source that would settle it.
- A shareable copy carries no internal facts, no personal implications, no references to the reader's own notes, and must reconstruct from the sources section alone.
