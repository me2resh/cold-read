# Pedalgrove, Velothane, Spokesmith and Rimwarden: who wins UK e-bike subscriptions, and what would change it

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Pedalgrove is second of four by fleet size and first by city coverage: about 11,000 bikes against Velothane's about 18,000, in 14 cities against Velothane's 6, 🟡 on the fleet figures because no operator publishes a count and these are the trade-press estimates squared with the bike asset lines in each company's accounts [^s4-1][^s1-1][^s1-4], and ✅ on the city counts, which both operators list on their own sites [^s2-3][^s2-5]. Its moat is the repair network, not the bikes: the only intangible asset it has ever booked is "service partner agreements", carried at GBP 1.4m in the FY2024 accounts, and the FY2023 accounts carried no intangible at all ✅ [^s1-1][^s1-3]. The leader, Velothane, is bigger on fleet and GBP 10 a month cheaper ✅ [^s2-1][^s2-4], but it is hiring for a repair capability it does not yet have: 14 of its 30 open roles are repair technicians or repair operations leads, every one posted since September 2025 ✅ [^s6-1][^s6-2][^s6-3]. Spokesmith owns its repair but runs 3 cities at GBP 79 a month, which caps how far it can grow on the current price ✅ [^s2-7]. Rimwarden is the cheapest and the smallest and says nothing about repair ❔ not on its pricing page, its terms page or its careers page [^s2-8][^s2-11][^s6-9]. The ranking changes on two things. The first is whether Velothane's repair hiring lands before its next raise, which would remove the one thing Pedalgrove does that Velothane does not 🟡, because the ads, a new subsidiary and a new repair hostname all point the same way but none of the three shows a shop open [^s6-1][^s1-10][^s5-2]. The second is whether the city grant scheme that favours operators with in-house repair is renewed in April 2026 on its current terms, which would favour Spokesmith and count against Pedalgrove's partner model 🟡, because the live guidance scores in-house repair and the renewal consultation has not published its new criteria [^s4-2][^s4-4].

## 3. The moat, as the record shows it

None of the four has made an acquisition, so there is no purchase price allocation to read ✅ [^s1-2][^s1-4][^s1-7][^s1-8]. The nearest thing is what each company has chosen to capitalise on its own balance sheet, and here the four diverge.

**Pedalgrove.** The FY2024 accounts, filed 30 September 2025, book one intangible asset, "service partner agreements", at GBP 1.4m cost with amortisation over three years ✅ [^s1-1]. The FY2023 accounts carried no intangible ✅ [^s1-3]. The note describes the asset as "onboarding payments and equipment provided to independent repair shops under partner agreements" ✅ [^s1-1]. The repair partners page lists 120 shops across 14 cities, read on 21 November 2025 ✅ [^s2-2]. The partner shop terms page says partners service Pedalgrove bikes "ahead of walk-in work during the agreed service window" and that the initial term is 24 months ✅ [^s2-12]. Whether partners are barred from servicing a competitor's fleet is ❔ not in the published shop terms or the FY2024 accounts note. The app's version history shows a "book a repair at a partner shop" feature shipped on 8 April 2025 ✅ [^s2-9]. The partners hostname resolves to a field-service software vendor, which fits a booking system shared with the shops 🟡, because a hostname shows that something exists and not how it is used [^s5-1]. The privacy page names the same vendor as a processor for "repair booking and partner scheduling", which confirms the use in the company's own words ✅ [^s5-6]. A chief operating officer was appointed on 12 May 2025, the first director added since incorporation ✅ [^s1-9]. The company's own board has 9 open roles, of which one is a "Partner Network Manager" for a region not yet on the cities page 🟡, read as a fifteenth city in preparation because the ad names a region the cities page does not [^s6-5][^s6-6][^s2-3]. The moat, as far as the record shows it, is 120 contracts with shops, each with a sunk onboarding payment and a 24-month term, in 14 cities where no competitor has a repair presence of any kind 🟡, because the competitor city lists and ad locations show no overlap outside the 6 cities Velothane serves [^s2-2][^s2-5][^s2-7][^s6-1].

**Velothane.** The FY2024 accounts book no intangible asset; the fixed assets are bikes, batteries and leasehold improvements ✅ [^s1-4]. The terms of service, in the version dated 1 February 2025, say repairs are "arranged through our service partners" ✅ [^s2-6]. The archived copy of the same page from 14 August 2024 says repairs are "carried out by our approved repairers", so the wording was softened before the CEO's June 2025 podcast and before the hiring began 🟡, a reading of two page versions rather than a statement by the company [^s3-6][^s2-6]. The Series B release of 19 March 2024 described the use of funds as "fleet expansion into three further cities" and did not mention repair ✅ [^s1-5]. A charge registered on 27 June 2025 secures asset finance over "e-bikes and associated batteries", which is consistent with a fleet financed rather than bought outright 🟡, because the charge document names the lender and the asset class but not the sum [^s1-6]. A subsidiary, Velothane Service Ltd, was incorporated on 9 October 2025 with the parent as sole shareholder and "repair and maintenance of bicycles" as its stated activity ✅ [^s1-10]. A certificate for a repair hostname under the company's domain was issued on 2 October 2025 ✅ [^s5-2]. The page source of the rider site loads a booking widget from a workshop-management vendor, added at some point between the August 2024 archived copy and the page read on 21 November 2025 ✅ [^s5-3][^s3-6]. The app's version history has no repair feature named in any release note ❔ not in the 14 release notes read [^s2-10]. The board, pulled from the applicant-tracking system's public API on 21 November 2025, lists 30 open roles: 12 repair technicians across 4 of its 6 cities, 2 repair operations leads, and 16 others ✅ [^s6-1][^s6-2][^s6-3][^s6-4]. An aggregator snippet shows a "Head of Repair" ad from August 2025 that is no longer on the board 🟡, from a snippet that could not be opened [^s6-8]. The moat Velothane has is price and fleet, and the record shows it building the thing it said was a commodity.

**Spokesmith.** The micro-entity accounts show one fixed-asset line and a long-term lease commitment described as "workshop premises" ✅ [^s1-7]. The how-it-works page says every repair is done by "our own mechanics in our own workshops" ✅ [^s2-7]. A workshop hostname under the company's domain has existed since 2023 ✅ [^s5-4]. The only open role found is a workshop technician posted on 11 August 2025 ✅ [^s6-7]. The moat is real and small: three workshops in three cities, and no sign in the record of a fourth 🟡, because the cities page, the accounts and the one ad all point at the same three places [^s2-7][^s1-7][^s6-7].

**Rimwarden.** The first accounts are micro-entity and show GBP 1.2m of fixed assets with no narrative ✅ [^s1-8]. The site runs on a hosted site builder with no subdomain beyond the marketing page 🟡, because the DNS shows a single CNAME to the builder's edge and nothing else [^s5-5]. The careers page read on 21 November 2025 lists no roles ✅ [^s6-9]. The repair model is ❔ not stated on the pricing page, the terms page or in the founder's one press interview [^s2-8][^s2-11][^s3-8].

**The base rate.** Two comparables from the record set the pattern. A European e-bike subscription operator that moved from contracted to in-house repair in 2022 said it took 14 months to cover its cities and that repair headcount rose to a fifth of staff 🟡, from a press interview and not from accounts [^s4-6]. A UK operator entered administration in November 2023 and the administrator's notice named "a backlog of bikes awaiting repair" as the reason subscribers cancelled ✅ [^s4-7]. The shared-scooter consolidation report of 2023 found that the operators which survived the 2022 to 2023 contraction were the ones with the lowest cost per repair, not the lowest price per ride 🟡, a finding from a different vehicle class applied here by analogy [^s4-5]. The pattern says repair is the cost line that decides survival, and that building it in-house takes about a year, which is the window Pedalgrove's partner network has to prove it can do the same job for less.

## 4. Regulatory climate

The city grant scheme is the one regulatory lever that moves the ranking. The scheme guidance of April 2024 scores applicants out of 100 and gives 15 points for "in-house repair capacity in the city", with partner arrangements scoring at most 5 of the 15 ✅ [^s4-2]. The 2024 to 2025 award list, published in July 2024, names Spokesmith with a full award in all three of its cities and Pedalgrove with a partial award in four of its cities ✅ [^s4-3]. Velothane is not on the award list, and the reason is ❔ not in the award list, the scheme guidance or any Velothane release read. The renewal consultation was published on 15 September 2025, closes on 12 December 2025, and says the renewed terms will be published in April 2026 ✅ [^s4-4]. The consultation asks whether "partner-based repair with service-level commitments" should score equally with in-house repair, which is the question that decides whether the scheme counts for or against Pedalgrove 🟡, because a consultation question is not a decision and the responses are not yet published [^s4-4]. The consultation does not say how much money is attached to the renewed scheme, and the source that would carry it is the authority's budget paper in the new financial year ❔ not in the consultation document or the 2024 guidance.

The scheme's reach matters as much as its scoring. The guidance lists the areas it covers, and laying that list against the three cities pages gives 5 of Pedalgrove's 14 cities, 3 of Velothane's 6 and all 3 of Spokesmith's, so a change in terms touches the leader's densest cities and the whole of Spokesmith 🟡, a count made from the area list and the cities pages rather than a figure any party has published [^s4-2][^s2-3][^s2-5][^s2-7]. Rimwarden's 2 cities are outside the scheme's area ✅ [^s4-2][^s2-8].

Two further rules apply to all four operators equally and do not change the ranking. The consumer regulator's April 2025 guidance on subscription contracts requires a cancellation route as easy as the sign-up route and a reminder before any renewal, and all four pricing pages now carry a cancellation line that matches it ✅ [^s4-8][^s2-1][^s2-4][^s2-7][^s2-8]. The March 2025 consultation on e-bike battery safety proposes a conformity requirement for batteries placed on the market and a duty to keep repair records for fleet operators ✅ [^s4-10]. If the record-keeping duty is adopted as drafted, an operator that controls its own repair records it once, and an operator that relies on 120 independent shops collects it 120 times 🟡, an inference from the draft duty and the partner model, not from anything the consultation says about either company [^s4-10][^s2-2].

Nothing in the record shows any of the four under enforcement, in a dispute with a city authority, or named in a safety recall ❔ not in the regulator's enforcement list, the four registry filing histories or the trade press read [^s1-2][^s4-8].

The Velothane board should be re-pulled monthly: if the 12 technician ads close and the two lead ads are refilled, the shops are open; if the technician ads are still open in March 2026 with first-published dates from September, they are evergreen requisitions and the plan has stalled 🟡, a reading rule rather than a fact about the company [^s6-1]. If none of the three events moves, the ranking on 21 November 2025 stands: Velothane first on fleet and price, Pedalgrove first on coverage and the only operator with repair booked as an asset, Spokesmith third and the only one the grant scheme scores in full, Rimwarden fourth and unread on repair 🟡, a ranking that rests on the fleet estimates in §2 and the filings in §3 [^s4-1][^s1-1][^s1-4][^s1-7][^s1-8].
